About

The 80% is loud. Revenue dips, customers vanish, numbers crash — you don't need an analyst to see that. The business already feels it. It's the other 20% that kills you: a slow leak of customers, fraud that never trips an alarm, problems that make no noise today and quietly grow until, by the time anyone notices, the damage is done. I call it the silent data. It doesn't announce itself, and that's exactly why it's dangerous. I go into the overlooked corners of the data and hunt these problems down while they're still small enough to fix. That's where I work.

Selected work

Each one started as something the business couldn't see. The charts use example numbers; the real figures stay with my employer.

Customers · churn

Every customer has their own normal

Most businesses call a customer "lost" after a fixed number of days, or judge them by the group they belong to. But a daily buyer who goes quiet for a week is a bigger worry than a monthly buyer who does the same.

What I did

I read each customer by their own buying habit. That shows who has stopped acting like themselves early enough to win them back, which a group average never shows.

Each customer

Each dot is a customer. Red dots have gone quiet for much longer than they usually do.

Customers · retention

The program that looked like a win

Customers in a premium program stayed much longer than everyone else, so the obvious plan was to spend more on it. First I checked whether they were simply better customers to begin with.

What I found

They were. Compared fairly, like with like, most of the gap disappeared, and the budget went where it would actually help.

Unfair comparison

Share of customers still with us after 90 days.

Operations · call centre

Missed calls, missed customers

People said the call centre missed "a few" calls. I counted how many calls came in each hour against how many the team could actually take.

What I found

At peak hours the calls outran the team, and the people we missed were mostly new customers trying to sign up.

Calls per hour

Above the dashed line, calls go unanswered.

Revenue · fraud

Usage that didn't add up

A handful of accounts were using the service in ways the pricing never allowed for. Hidden among thousands of normal accounts, they made no noise.

What I did

I built checks that flag these accounts automatically, so they get reviewed before the losses grow.

Use vs. what the plan allows

Each bar is one account. The red ones are far past their plan.

Personal project · data engineering

A data warehouse, built from scratch

To understand the whole journey data takes, I built one end to end: raw data comes in, gets cleaned in layers, and ends up ready for reports.

The point

It runs itself every day, and it stops on its own if the data fails a quality check, so bad numbers never reach a report.
